# TESS Program G09058

Title: Origin, Evolution, And Fate Of Hot Jupiters From TESS Photometry

PI: Petigura, Erik - University Of California (Los Angeles)

Type: SMALL

Summary: Hot Jupiters likely form through at least two channels: disk-driven migration, which produces companions near mean-motion resonance, and high-eccentricity migration, which produces isolated planets that tidally circularize. We propose a systematic analysis of TESS light curves of confirmed and candidate giant planets to probe both channels and the ultimate fate of hot Jupiters. Using transit timing variations (TTVs), we will detect nearby companions and constrain their masses and eccentricities. Using the photoeccentric effect, we will identify high-eccentricity proto-hot-Jupiters. We will also search for quadratic timing trends indicative of tidal orbital decay. Cycle 9 is ideal: baselines reaching ~9 years enable robust TTV waveform characterization for the first time.
